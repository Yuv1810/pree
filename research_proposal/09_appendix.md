## Appendix D: Kessler Psychological Distress Scale (K10; Kessler et al., 2002)

---

> **Note:** The K10 is in the public domain and is freely available for use in research.

**Instructions:** The following questions ask about how you have been feeling during the **past 30 days**. For each question, select the response that best describes how often you had this feeling.

| 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| None of the time | A little of the time | Some of the time | Most of the time | All of the time |

### Items

| # | Item |
|---|------|
| 1 | During the last 30 days, about how often did you feel **tired out for no good reason**? |
| 2 | During the last 30 days, about how often did you feel **nervous**? |
| 3 | During the last 30 days, about how often did you feel **so nervous that nothing could calm you down**? |
| 4 | During the last 30 days, about how often did you feel **hopeless**? |
| 5 | During the last 30 days, about how often did you feel **restless or fidgety**? |
| 6 | During the last 30 days, about how often did you feel **so restless you could not sit still**? |
| 7 | During the last 30 days, about how often did you feel **depressed**? |
| 8 | During the last 30 days, about how often did you feel that **everything was an effort**? |
| 9 | During the last 30 days, about how often did you feel **so sad that nothing could cheer you up**? |
| 10 | During the last 30 days, about how often did you feel **worthless**? |

### Scoring Guide

| Score Range | Interpretation |
|:-----------:|----------------|
| 10–15 | Low — Likely to be well |
| 16–21 | Moderate — Likely to have a mild mental disorder |
| 22–29 | High — Likely to have a moderate mental disorder |
| 30–50 | Very High — Likely to have a severe mental disorder |

**Total Score** = Sum of all 10 items (Range: 10–50)

| Very Strongly Disagree | Strongly Disagree | Mildly Disagree | Neutral | Mildly Agree | Strongly Agree | Very Strongly Agree |

### Items

## Appendix F: Debriefing Sheet

---

### DEBRIEFING FORM

**Study Title:** Self-Compassion and Psychological Distress among University Students: The Moderating Role of Perceived Social Support

Thank you for participating in this study!

**What was this study about?**

This study aims to understand the relationship between how you treat yourself during difficult times (self-compassion), emotional distress (psychological distress), and the support you perceive from family, friends, and significant others (perceived social support). Specifically, we are examining whether social support influences the strength of the relationship between self-compassion and psychological distress.

**Why is this research important?**

Understanding how self-compassion and social support work together to protect against psychological distress can help universities develop better mental health programs and support services for students.

Your responses are completely anonymous and confidential. They will only be used for research purposes and will not be shared with anyone outside the research team.
